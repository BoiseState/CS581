# Layer 1 Reading Notes

- I was surprised by how much the readings frame nuclear cybersecurity as an organizational and economic problem, not just a technical hardening problem. Anderson's point that attackers optimize for the lowest-cost path changes how I read RG 5.71: the Cyber Security Program has to make bypass, insider misuse, and maintenance shortcuts costly enough that they stop being the attractive path.

- The distinction between safety and security was sharper than I expected. Safety engineering can assume random failure distributions, but security has to assume an adversary will search for the precise assumption the plant depends on. That matters in nuclear environments because many safety systems are designed to be predictable and stable, which can also make their defensive assumptions predictable.

- RG 5.71's focus on Critical Digital Assets surprised me because the CDA boundary is not self-evident. NEI 08-09 makes the licensee's CDA determination process central, which means the quality of the program depends heavily on local classification judgment before any control is applied.

- I did not expect legacy systems to be treated so explicitly as a normal implementation constraint. NEI 08-09's compensating-controls logic recognizes that some systems cannot be patched, instrumented, or re-architected without creating operational or licensing problems.

- I remain uncertain about how consistently inspectors can evaluate CDA determination quality across plants. If two facilities make different but plausible boundary decisions, the documents do not make clear how much variation is acceptable before it becomes a program weakness.

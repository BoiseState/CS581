# Synthesis Note

My Layer 1 read surfaced the importance of classification, incentives, and operational constraint. Before these readings, it would be easy to treat nuclear cybersecurity as a list of technical controls applied to important systems. The readings instead show that the first hard problem is deciding which assets and dependencies matter, and the second hard problem is applying controls in an environment where safety, reliability, regulatory commitments, and legacy equipment limit the available options.

The LLM synthesis added a broader connection between Anderson's security economics and the nuclear regulatory material. It helped frame defense in depth as an economic and adversarial strategy: raise attacker cost, reduce available paths, increase detection opportunities, and limit consequences when a control fails. It also highlighted that NEI 08-09's compensating controls are not a loophole by themselves. They are a practical response to legacy systems, but they need evidence and periodic validation to avoid becoming paper risk acceptance.

The NRC Regulator and Plant Cybersecurity Manager perspectives diverged most around evidence versus implementability. The regulator wants to know whether the program can withstand inspection: CDA determinations, boundary controls, procedures, training, records, and corrective actions must be traceable and defensible. The cybersecurity manager wants controls that can survive the plant environment: they must fit outage schedules, vendor constraints, qualified equipment limits, safety procedures, and staff workload.

That divergence raises an operational question neither Anderson nor the regulatory documents fully answer: how should a plant decide when a cybersecurity improvement is worth the operational and safety risk of changing a stable legacy environment? Anderson explains why adversaries exploit weak assumptions, and RG 5.71/NEI 08-09 explain the program structure, but neither gives a simple method for balancing cyber risk reduction against the risk introduced by modifying nuclear operational systems.

# Week 1 Example Student Work — Jordan Reyes

This folder contains example Week 1 deliverables written from the perspective of a fictional student persona. It is provided so you can calibrate expectations — not as a template to copy, but as a model of the *process* as much as the output.

---

## Who Is Jordan?

**Jordan Reyes** is a second-year MS Computer Science student. Before grad school, Jordan spent three years as a backend engineer at a fintech startup — building payment processing pipelines, dealing with PCI-DSS compliance audits, and learning that security requirements written by lawyers are often at odds with what engineers can actually implement on deadline.

Jordan signed up for CS 581 for two reasons: (1) a genuine interest in critical infrastructure security after reading Kim Zetter's *Countdown to Zero Day* the summer before, and (2) a vague sense that "ICS security" was where the interesting problems were.

Jordan has **zero nuclear background**. On day one, the phrase "10 CFR 73.54" reads as noise. By the end of Week 2, it will not.

**AI tool:** Claude Code  
**Chosen system (W1):** DCS — Digital Control System (Purdue Level 2)  
**Why DCS:** It's the operational heart of the plant and the closest analogue to the distributed systems Jordan already understands from fintech. Also: the Plant Architecture Diagram's affinity rating marks DCS as Primary for W1, and Jordan decides to trust the diagram.

---

## A Note on the Session Log

The session log (`session-log.md`) is the most important file in this folder. Read it before the other deliverables. It shows:

- A first prompt that produces nothing useful
- The moment Jordan realizes they need to read RG 5.71 before prompting again
- A factual error the AI makes that Jordan catches
- The point where the "What This Map Cannot Tell You" section becomes the most interesting part of the exercise

The attack surface map and system selection documents are the *outputs*. The session log is the *work*.

---

## What This Example Is Not

- It is not a perfect submission. Jordan makes choices you might make differently.
- It is not the only right approach to W1. Another student who chose RPS would produce a completely different — and equally valid — attack surface map.
- It is not a ceiling. If your W1 work goes substantially deeper than Jordan's, that is a good sign.

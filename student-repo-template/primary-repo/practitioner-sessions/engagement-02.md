## Practitioner Background

Two or three sentences. Who is this person, what do they actually do, and how did they arrive in
nuclear cybersecurity work? Write it from what you heard rather than from their bio page.

## Key Insight

One paragraph. What did they say that actually shifts how you think about the field? Not the most
interesting moment, the one that changes something. If nothing shifted, explain why and what you
expected to hear instead.

## Connection to Your System

One paragraph. You were working a specific nuclear system when this session happened. What does
their experience tell you about that system? Where does it apply directly, and where does your
plant context require you to adjust their framing?

## Where It Confirms, Challenges, or Extends the Course Material

One or two paragraphs, and this is the section the grade turns on. Name a specific reading,
regulatory framework, or workshop finding, and say whether the practitioner's real-world
experience confirms it, contradicts it, or takes it somewhere the source did not go. Cite both
sides: what the source says, and what the practitioner said. A summary with no tension in it
scores in the 70s.

## Knowledge Gap Identified

One paragraph. What did this session reveal that you do not yet understand well enough? Name it
specifically: a concept, regulation, protocol, or technology. Then describe how you would use an
AI tool to close that gap, what you would verify it against, and how you would know when you
understood it.

## AI Tool Reflection

Did you use an AI tool to prepare, process what you heard, or follow up? What did you ask, what
did it get right, and where did you correct it?

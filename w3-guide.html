3 The pymodbus traps

The lab host gives you pymodbus 3.15. Almost every example on the internet, and almost everything an AI assistant will generate, targets 2.x and will not run. These five cost real time to find:

1. ModbusSlaveContext does not exist. Renamed to ModbusDeviceContext, and that whole datastore API is deprecated and disappears in pymodbus 4. Use SimData / SimDevice.

2. ModbusSequentialDataBlock(0, ...) raises TypeError: 0 <= address < 65535, despite what the message says. Do not fight the legacy path.

3. SimDevice wants a tuple, not a list of lists, in the order (di, co, hr, ir). A list raises simdata=list[0] is not a SimData entry.

4. srv.context is a SimCore. Its only accessors are async_getValues and async_setValues, both coroutines, and the order is (device_id, func_code, address, ...). device_id must match your SimDevice id.

5. Do not wrap those accessors in a bare except. A silent failure there means your values never move, and that is a graded check. The reference build made exactly this mistake and failed its own grading the first time.

4 Write your point map

Six to twelve points. Address, name, type, units, range, read or write, and what it physically represents. Your W1 attack surface map already names the interfaces; this is that map made concrete.

Two things people get wrong. Modbus registers are 16-bit integers with no decimal point, so a temperature is stored scaled (274 means 27.4 °C) and the scale factor lives only in your documentation. And wire addresses are zero-based while the conventional 40001 numbering is one-based. State both explicitly or your own client will misread your own system.

5 Make the values move

A server that answers correctly but returns the same number every time passes every other check and fails this one. It is worth two of the ten Lab Build points, and it is the check most people will fail.

Give the process a reason to change. In the reference build, commanding a pump raises flow, flow raises header pressure, and higher flow drops the supply temperature until it crosses an alarm limit. That coupling means a two-minute capture contains a causal chain rather than a set of unrelated readings, which is what makes the protocol analysis writable.

6 Put basic auth on your HMI

Required. An unauthenticated request must return 401, and the probe checks exactly that. Use hmac.compare_digest, not ==: string comparison leaks timing.

Notice the asymmetry while you do it. Your HMI can have a password because HTTP can carry one. Your Modbus service cannot, because the protocol has nowhere to put a credential. That is the finding of the week, and W4 is where you design what sits around it.

7 Emit events to the OT-SIEM

Minimum: service.state on start and stop, every protocol.write, sampled protocol.read, auth.success and auth.failure from your HMI, and a process.alarm.

8 Run it under systemd, then capture

mkdir -p ~/.config/systemd/user
cp workshops/week-03/lab/units/*.service ~/.config/systemd/user/
systemctl --user daemon-reload
systemctl --user enable --now plant-<yoursystem> plant-<yoursystem>-hmi
systemctl --user status plant-<yoursystem>

Lingering is already on for your account, so services survive logout and reboot. The probe checks that they come back.

Then capture your own traffic:

sudo tcpdump -i lo -w ~/capture.pcap port 5051
# from your laptop, to view an HMI:
ssh -N -L 5050:127.0.0.1:5050 dcs@plant.sanctumsec.com
Wireshark will not dissect Modbus on a non-standard port. Your service is not on 502, so use Decode As → Modbus/TCP. Discovering that is a better lesson about port-based assumptions than any paragraph.
